Routing and transfer audit, 14 November 2026

Verdict: the revised padding argument does not close the full proof. This audit
found concrete defects in the bulk potential certificate and the general
transfer lemma, and a missing parameter-coverage argument in Lemma N. These
findings are independent of whether the manuscript is peer reviewed or formally
verified. They are not counterexamples to the claimed NP-completeness theorem.

1. The Bulk Lemma's uniform Omega certificate fails at a valid reduction size.

Section 8.3 defines Omega using the fixed coefficient 10^4 and offset 10^9.
It claims the same ten local maxima and their six-segment escape certificates
work for every m. In particular, S2 claims the Omega comparisons are preserved
when a vertex neighbourhood is transferred to a larger octahedron.

Consider these three segments near the -y vertex, in absolute coordinates:

  P = {(0,4,0), (1,3,-1)}
  Q = {(-1,3,1)}
  R = {(2,4,-2)}.

The displayed foliation, merge rules, and Appendix B's -y patch make Q and R
the two joint-neighbours of P. Q is a row segment; P and R are not. Their
potentials are

  Omega(P) = 10^4(2m-7) + 1,
  Omega(Q) = 10^9 + 2,
  Omega(R) = 10^4(2m-9) + 4.

At m=30, P is not a local maximum. At m=50034 the values are respectively
1,000,610,001; 1,000,000,002; and 1,000,590,004. P is therefore a new local
maximum. A direct local reconstruction traces both directions for six segment
transitions: neither reaches an endpoint nor a larger-potential segment.
This is a failure of the stated certificate, not merely a missing rerun.

The large size is in the actual reduction family: K=1, B=252 produces m=50034
under Section 6. The witness was checked directly at both m=30 and m=50034,
without enumerating the large octahedron or assuming the disputed transfer.
Run: node research/tetrahedral/audit_transfer_witnesses.mjs

The analogous +y neighbourhood also acquires a new maximum. Geometry alone
does not preserve numerical potential comparisons. A plausible repair is an
explicit m-dependent offset or a lexicographic potential with row segments
above all non-row segments, followed by a proof that the resulting comparisons
and escape certificates hold uniformly. That repair has not been made here.

2. Lemma N's stated base cases are outside its prescribed parameter family.

Combining the constants in Section 6 gives

  m = (K+2)(18K+48)B + 38K + 100.

For positive K and B, the smallest value is m=336, at K=B=1. The stated bases
m=80,82 cannot be whole instances with those constants. The appendix mentions
experiments with smaller J,R,C, but no theorem relates those altered instances
to every instance in the prescribed family.

Likewise, increasing K at fixed B changes C, J, R and all existing separator
positions: j_k increases by 18kB+18. It is not just the insertion of one slot
and one separator in an otherwise unchanged instance. The manuscript needs a
parameterized statement, probably with independent J,C,R subject to explicit
separation bounds, and a proof that every relevant labelled neighbourhood is
represented among certified templates. Alternatively it needs induction bases
and steps that actually belong to the family being proved.

The radius-five signature checks do not by themselves certify a construction
whose frame-link rule is stated to depend on radius eleven. A complete transfer
must preserve all data used by that rule, including cover ends, slot and corner
labels, cap/PPP choices, and borrowed-column exclusions. A hand argument could
supply this; the displayed argument does not establish that coverage.

3. Lemma T is false with its stated unrestricted hypotheses.

Take m=11, R=0, R'=10 and v=(0,30,10). Its centre is (0,20,0), so Y=10 and Z=9.
The condition 4m >= 3R+2R'+24 holds with equality. All eight depths are

  (0,40,20,20; 41,1,21,21),

so v belongs to Oct(11). Both y and z are excluded from the eligible axes,
leaving x. Taking the expressly allowed positive sign gives

  tau(v)=v-2e_x-(0,2,0)=(-2,28,10).

Its depths relative to Oct(10) are

  (0,40,16,16; 37,-3,21,21).

A depth of 1 decreased to -3, although the lemma allows decreases only for
depths greater than R+4=4. The image has left the smaller octahedron. The negative
x choice fails as well. This counterexample is checked in the same witness
script. The general lemma must be strengthened or restricted, and applications
must satisfy the corrected hypotheses. This counterexample alone does not
disprove a narrower transfer statement sufficient for the construction.

4. What checked out in this audit, and the limits of those checks.

The separation arguments in Lemma F(A-C) are consistent once the near field
supplies disjoint available column tails. In particular, equal-level avenues
in the same quadrant have disjoint alpha strips; descending level order keeps
radial arms clear of higher avenues; and private slabs and private joining
levels separate the level-curve approaches and joins. Borrowed-column
availability is still a near-field obligation, not a consequence of F.

An independent coordinate reconstruction used all ordinary dangling columns
for m=3,4,6, with three different pairings at each size. All 366 resulting
far-field routes (4,234,194 P-site occurrences across nine configurations)
passed lattice adjacency, self-avoidance, mutual disjointness, exclusion from
Oct(m), the stated slab bounds, and the new base-length bound.
Run: node research/tetrahedral/audit_far_field.mjs

A separate implementation of the bulk rules and the actual Appendix B patches
at m=9,10,14,24,30 reproduced the claimed damaged-end and merge counts. Every
component was a path with valid surface ends and at least six sites; no cycle
or forbidden second-row run end was found. This supports the proposed cover
while exposing the different, unbounded-size failure of its Omega certificate.
Run: node research/tetrahedral/audit_bulk.mjs

These tests do not exhaust all near-field configurations or prove the bulk
cover for all sizes. No claim that a cycle or an impossible fold was found is
made. The demonstrated defects are in the universal arguments currently used
to establish correctness of the construction.
