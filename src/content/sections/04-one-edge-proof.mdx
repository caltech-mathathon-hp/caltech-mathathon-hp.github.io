<Theorem kind="Theorem" name="bins on one edge">
From every bin packing puzzle with even sizes we can build, in time polynomial in its size written in unary, an HP string with $n^3$ H's that has a perfect fold on the cubic lattice $\mathbb Z^3$ exactly when the items fit. Here $n$ grows linearly with the sum of the sizes.
</Theorem>

[The original proof](../original-proof/) makes its bins out of strips of one face of the cube. Walls cut the strips out, paddings and plugs give them the right size, and the items are ribbons that fill them. Here the bins are gaps on one edge of the cube. Long rods, the separators, hang on that edge with $B$ nodes between each two, and each item is a rod that fills part of a gap. Two walls fix the two ends of the row of separators, and one ribbon fills the rest of the face.

The board has the layout of the original one. Cards [2](#step-ours-problems), [3](#step-ours-cube), [5](#step-ours-local-rules) and [6](#step-ours-skeleton) are the original steps, restated for our string. The new steps are [7](#step-ours-working-face)–[11](#step-ours-packing) on the left and [13](#step-ours-frame)–[16](#step-ours-item-routes) on the right. The new idea is in [10 Every gap holds B](#step-ours-gaps): the P's between two separators cannot reach around a gap of more than $B$ nodes, because the rods in the gap have already taken the nodes just outside it.

Each card opens on the idea of its step and its claim in words, with the exact statement, the proof and our notes under "The details". One step gets three dots: [15 Seven corner routes](#step-ours-corner-routes), whose case analysis is long.

## Folding a small instance

The 3D view runs a shorter version of this construction. The cube, the frame and the bins on one edge stay, but the pieces on that edge are simpler. Each separator is a single H between two P's, and each item is a run of H's lying along the edge. Instead of the two walls, two runs of P's that are only just long enough to reach across a face pin the first and the last separator to the two ends of the edge, so every bin is forced to be exactly full. We call this edge the *shelf* and these runs of P's *tethers*.

The view opens on a small instance, two bins of size 2 and items 1, 1, 2, already folded: the H's fill an $n\times n\times n$ cube and the bottom front edge, the shelf, carries the packing. Press <kbd>←</kbd> or drag along the bar under the cube to go back through the steps. Or choose another instance and drag its items into the bins, or let the solver pack them, and press **Fold the string**: $S$ appears as a straight chain, and every press of <kbd>→</kbd> folds its next piece. The [original proof](../original-proof/#folding-a-small-instance) has the same view for Berger and Leighton's string.

### Spelling the string

Before anything else the instance is normalised: $B$ and every size are multiplied by 6, and if the number of bins $m$ is even, one item of size $B$ is added with a bin of its own, so that $m$ becomes odd. Then $n = m(B+1)+3$ and, with $k$ items of sizes $a_1,\dots,a_k$,

$$
S = H^{N_0}\,PP\,H(PPH)^{9n-11}\,P^{\lambda}\,H(PPH)^{n-3}\,P^{n}\,H(P^{B+6}H)^{m}\,P^{n}\,H(PPH)^{n-3}\,P^{\lambda}H(PPH)^{a_1-1}\cdots P^{\lambda}H(PPH)^{a_k-1}\,P,
$$

where each $H(PPH)^{r-1}$ is a *dense run* of $r$ H's, each between two P's, $N_0 = n^3-12n+16$ and $\lambda = 4n+2k$. Every H of a dense run, and every separator, has P's on both sides, so in a perfect fold it lies on an edge of the cube. There are exactly $12n-16$ of them, as many as the edges have nodes, and the core $H^{N_0}$ fills the rest.

Even the smallest instances give a sizeable cube: two bins of size 2 become three bins of size 12, so $n = 42$, with 74,088 H's and about 76,000 letters. One bin of size 2 already gives $n = 16$, which is among the presets.

### How close this is to our write-up

The string is exactly the formula above, checked letter by letter, and the core, the dense runs, the tethers and the items sit where our write-up of this version puts them: the core fills the cube layer by layer, and each dense run lies along its edge. One kind of route we had to choose ourselves. Each linker $P^{\lambda}$ goes straight down to a level of its own below the cube and comes back up, and our write-up uses up its surplus length with a rectangle in front of the cube. Here the surplus is folded into a flat meander under the cube, on the same level, so that nothing hangs in front of the shelf.

Every fold is checked as it is built: its letters are those of $S$, no site is used twice, the H's fill the cube, and the H–H pairs reach $3n^2(n-1)$. The panel on the left lists these checks.

## Glossary

The words of [the original glossary](../original-proof/#glossary) keep their meaning: perfect fold, packing, rod, ribbon, core, face ribbon, corner rod, tenth-edge rod, free edges, front face, wall, snake and skeleton. This table adds the new words, in the order of [4 Spelling the string](#step-ours-string).

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| anchor edge | the free edge that holds the single and the anchor rod |
| bin edge | the other free edge, which holds the separators and the item rods |
| single | the rod <Seq s="PHP" /> with one H, at one end of the anchor edge |
| separator | a rod of $B+6$ H's on the bin edge; there are $K+1$ |
| spacer | the run of $B+2$ P's between two consecutive separators |
| gap | the nodes of the bin edge between two consecutive separators; a gap plays a bin |
| anchor rod | the rod of $n-3$ H's that fills the anchor edge but for the single |
| filler | the ribbon of $(n-2)(n-4)$ H's that fills the front face between the two walls |
| item rod | a rod with as many H's as the size of its item, <Seq s="(PHP)^{s(u)}" /> |
| linker | a run of $8n$ P's between two large pieces; $\lvert U\rvert+6$ of them |
| $g$ | the length of a separator, $B+6$ |
| $n$ | the side of the cube, $(K+1)(B+6)+KB+2$ |

## What we checked

- **A perfect fold gives a packing.** Cards 5–11 give the whole argument.
- **A packing gives a perfect fold.** Cards 12–16 give every coordinate and every route, from our notes on the construction. A coordinate checker built the whole fold, letter by letter, for five instances with up to 1,825,956 letters. It checked that consecutive letters are neighbours, that no node is used twice, that the H's fill the cube, and both counts of contacts. It also confirmed by search the shortest walks around gaps of 0 to 12 nodes used in [10 Every gap holds B](#step-ours-gaps). These checks support the proofs on the cards; they do not replace them.
