## Folding

Place the letters of the string one at a time, each on a free node next to the one before. Two H's
that end up side by side without being neighbours in the string make a **contact**, drawn as a
dashed line. Each level says how many contacts its best fold has; try to reach that number.

<div class="wide">
  <FoldGame client:load levels latticePicker lattice="cubic" />
</div>

The levels start on the cubic lattice, where Berger and Leighton proved their theorem, and lead
from a first contact to a cube of 27 H's. The switch and the tabs at the top change the lattice,
and under Own string you can fold any string you type. The [front page](../) explains why the H's
of a best fold gather into a cube, and [Context](../context/) says what is known about folding on
each lattice.

## Bin packing

**Bin packing** gives us some items, each with a size, and some bins of equal size, and asks
whether the items fill the bins exactly.

<div class="wide">
  <BinPack client:visible levels={['fill-two-bins', 'dead-end', 'three-bins', 'three-sixes', 'running-example']} />
</div>

For each bin packing puzzle, Berger and Leighton write down one HP string that has a perfect fold
exactly when the items fit. [The original proof](../original-proof/) shows how. Its figures use the
last level, *The running example*.
