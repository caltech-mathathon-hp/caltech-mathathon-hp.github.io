### The idea

In a perfect fold every P lies outside the cube, so a P next to an H in the string must sit on a neighbour of that H outside the cube. A node inside the cube has no such neighbour. A node in the middle of a face has one, and a node on an edge or at a corner has two or three. This is the principle behind the whole string: an H between two P's can only sit on an edge or at a corner, an H with one P beside it only on the surface, and only an H with no P beside it can go inside.

So we spell three kinds of piece. A **rod** has its H's alone between P's, as in <Seq s="PHPPHPPHP" />, which we write <Seq s="(PHP)^3" />: an exponent means repetition. The number of H's is the rod's **length**. A **ribbon** such as <Seq s="(PHHP)^3" /> has its H's two at a time, so each H has one P beside it. The **core** is one long run of H's with a P at each end. We give the string exactly as many H's of each kind as the cube has places for them: rods for the edges and corners, ribbons for the faces, and the core for the inside.

Two edges must stay empty, because the walls that make the bins will hang between them. So the string starts with a rod so long that it has only one way to lie. This **corner rod** passes every corner and covers nine edges, and a second rod covers a tenth ([6 Two edges stay free](#step-bl-skeleton)). The two edges left are the **free edges**, and the face between them is the **front face**. A **wall** is a ribbon as long as a row of the front face, a row being the line of nodes from one free edge to the other. Each wall hangs between rods on the free edges: short rods of $q$ H's and long rods of $2q+1$. These two lengths have no common factor, and that pins the rods to one pattern ([7 One length per edge](#step-bl-free-edges)). The wall pieces come in **periods** of a short rod, a wall, a long rod, a wall and a short rod, and the two walls of a period enclose a **strip**. A last rod, the **spaced rod**, fills a few holes that the periods leave ([11 Building the walls](#step-bl-build-walls)).

The strips play the bins. A strip consists of whole rows, though, so its size $T$ is set by the cube and not by $B$. To fix this, each strip also gets a ribbon of $T-B$ H's, which leaves exactly $B$ nodes free. We call it the strip's **padding**. We need at least $K$ strips so that every bin gets one, and we close the spare strips with ribbons of $B$ H's, called **plugs**. Each item becomes an **item ribbon** with as many H's as its size. A ribbon has an even number of H's, which is why the sizes and $B$ are even. Before each padding, plug and item ribbon comes a run of $52n+2$ P's. We call it a **linker**, and it is long enough to reach from any strip to any other ([14 Loops take up slack](#step-bl-linkers)).

Last, the sizes. We want at least $K$ strips, one per bin, and we want every strip to hold more than $2B$ nodes, because then a padding fills more than half a strip and no strip can hold two ([9 Reading off a packing](#step-bl-packing)). Under *The details* we choose $q$, and with it the cube's side $n$ and the strip size $T$, so that both hold.

### Statement

The string $S$ has exactly $n^3$ H's, with as many of each kind as the cube has places for them, and about $n^3$ letters in all.

### Exact statement

We spell the string $S$ in three parts: a **frame** that fills all of the cube but the front face, **walls** that cut that face into strips, and an **item part** that fills the strips. A **rod** <Seq s="(PHP)^k" /> holds $k$ **lone H's**, each between two P's, and we call it a $k$-rod. A **ribbon** <Seq s="(PHHP)^k" /> holds $2k$ H's, each with one P beside it. We set $q=2\max\big(K,\lceil B^{1/3}\rceil\big)$, $n=q(2q+1)+2$ and $T=(2q-1)(n-2)$. In string order, $S$ consists of:

- **Frame.** The **corner rod**, with $9n-10$ lone H's; a **face ribbon** of $(n-2)^2$ H's; the **tenth-edge rod**, with $n-2$ lone H's; three more face ribbons and a fifth that is two H's short; and the **core**, a run of $(n-2)^3+2$ H's.
- **Walls.** $q$ **periods**, one per strip. Each is a $q$-rod, a **wall**, a $(2q+1)$-rod, a second wall and a second $q$-rod, where a wall is a ribbon of $n-2$ H's. Then the **spaced rod**: $q$ lone H's with $2q+6$ P's between each two.
- **Item part.** $q$ **paddings**, ribbons of $T-B$ H's; $q-K$ **plugs**, ribbons of $B$ H's; and an **item ribbon** of $s(u)$ H's for each item $u$. Each of these ribbons is joined to the piece before it by a **linker**, a run of $52n+2$ P's.

Short runs of P's join the other pieces, as listed in *The exact string* below. The string has these properties:

1. $q$, $n$ and $T$ are even, $q\ge4$ and $n\ge38$.
2. $T>2B$, so $2(T-B)>T$, and two paddings never fit in one strip.
3. $S$ has $12n-16$ lone H's, $6(n-2)^2-2$ H's in ribbons and $(n-2)^3+2$ H's in the core: $n^3$ H's in all.
4. $S$ has $n^3+O(n^2+n|U|)$ letters, where $|U|$ is the number of items and $n=O(K^2+B^{2/3})$.
5. No P of $S$ has an H on both sides.

### Choosing the numbers

A period takes $2q+1$ rows: its two walls and the $2q-1$ rows of its strip. Add the top and bottom rows, which lie on edges of the cube, and the cube has side $n=q(2q+1)+2$, while a strip has $T=(2q-1)(n-2)$ nodes. We choose $q=2\max\big(K,\lceil B^{1/3}\rceil\big)$. It is at least $K$, so every bin gets a strip. It is at least $2B^{1/3}$, which makes a strip hold more than $2B$ nodes. A padding then fills more than half a strip, and no strip can hold two ([9 Reading off a packing](#step-bl-packing)). And it is even, so a row has an even number of nodes and a wall can fill it two H's at a time.

The core's two end H's each have a P beside them, so they too sit on a face. That is why one of the five face ribbons is two H's short: it leaves room for them.

### The exact string

With $c=52$, the three parts of $S$ are, in this order:

- **Frame:** the corner rod <Seq s="(PHP)^{9n−10}" />, a face ribbon <Seq s="(PHHP)^{(n−2)²/2}" />, the tenth-edge rod <Seq s="(PHP)^{n−2}" />, <Seq s="P^4" />, three more face ribbons each followed by <Seq s="P^7" />, a face ribbon one block short, and the core <Seq s="PH^{(n−2)³+2}P^{8+q}" />.
- **Walls:** $q$ periods, each a $q$-rod, a wall <Seq s="(PHHP)^{(n−2)/2}" />, a $(2q+1)$-rod, <Seq s="P^4" />, a wall, a $q$-rod and <Seq s="P^{2q}" />; then the spaced rod <Seq s="PH(P^{2q+6}H)^{q−1}P" />.
- **Item part:** $q$ paddings <Seq s="(PHHP)^{(T−B)/2}" />, $q-K$ plugs <Seq s="(PHHP)^{B/2}" />, and one item ribbon <Seq s="(PHHP)^{s(u)/2}" /> for each item $u$, each of these ribbons preceded by <Seq s="P^{cn}" />.

### Uses

[2 Why bin packing](#step-bl-problems): the instance $U$, $s$, $K$, $B$.

### Proof

1. $q$ is even by definition, so $n-2=q(2q+1)$ and $T$ are even. Every size is at least 2, so either $K\ge2$, or $K=1$ and $B=\sum_u s(u)\ge2$. In both cases $q\ge4$, and then $n\ge4\cdot9+2$.
2. $q\ge2B^{1/3}$ gives $q^3\ge8B$. Since $T=(2q-1)q(2q+1)$, which is $4q^3-q\ge3q^3$, we get $T\ge24B>2B$.
3. Lone H's: $9n-10$ and $n-2$ in the frame, $q+(2q+1)+q$ in each period, $q$ in the spaced rod. With $q(4q+2)=2(n-2)$ the total is $12n-16$. Ribbon H's: the face ribbons have $5(n-2)^2-2$. The walls have $2q(n-2)$. The item part has $q(T-B)+(q-K)B+KB=qT$. Since $2q+q(2q-1)=q(2q+1)=n-2$, the walls and the item part together have $(n-2)^2$. Core: $(n-2)^3+2$. With $m=n-2$, the sum is $12m+8+6m^2+m^3$, which is $(m+2)^3=n^3$.
4. Count the P's. Rods have two per lone H, ribbons one per H, and the runs <Seq s="P^4" />, <Seq s="P^7" />, <Seq s="P^{8+q}" />, <Seq s="P^{2q}" /> and those of the spaced rod have $O(q^2)$ P's in all. That is $O(n^2)$, plus $2q-K+|U|$ runs <Seq s="P^{cn}" />, and $q\le n$. Finally $n=8M^2+2M+2$ with $M=\max(K,\lceil B^{1/3}\rceil)$.
5. Every piece listed above begins and ends with P, and inside a piece no P has an H on both sides.

### Our note

The plugs <Seq s="(PHHP)^{B/2}" /> need $B$ even, and the paper does not say so. Nothing is lost: if $B$ is odd the answer is no, since every set of items has an even total, and the instances made from 3-PARTITION in [2 Why bin packing](#step-bl-problems) have $B$ even. The bounds $q\ge4$ and $T\ge24B$ are ours; later cards use $q\ge4$.
