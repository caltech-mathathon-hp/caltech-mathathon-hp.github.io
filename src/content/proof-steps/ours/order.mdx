### The idea

The separators are rods of $B+6$ H's, and between two consecutive ones the string has $B+4$ P's, so their facing ends are at most $B+5$ steps apart. Now look at a separator that ends at some row of the bin edge. The next separator is a run of $B+6$ nodes that does not overlap it. If it lay on the other side, or on this side but run backwards, the end it starts with would be at least $B+6$ rows away. So it must start just past the end of this one and run in the same direction.

So all $K+1$ separators lie along the bin edge in string order, all in one direction. From the first H of the first to the last H of the last they span at least $(K+1)(B+6)-1$ rows, far more than 1. By [8 Walls fix the ends](#step-ours-anchors) that span is then $n-3$ rows: the separators run from one end of the bin edge to the other. What they leave free are $K$ gaps, one between each two consecutive separators, with $KB$ nodes in all.

### Statement

The separators lie in string order along the whole bin edge, all in the same direction, and leave $K$ gaps between them with $KB$ nodes in all.

### Exact statement

In a perfect fold, in the coordinates of [8 Walls fix the ends](#step-ours-anchors):

1. the $K+1$ separators fill disjoint runs of the bin edge, which follow each other along the edge in string order, all traversed in the same direction;
2. the first separator starts at one end of the bin edge, $j=1$ or $j=n-2$, and the last ends at the other;
3. the nodes of the bin edge outside the separators form $K$ **gaps**, one between each two consecutive separators, with $KB$ nodes in all, and the item rods fill them.

### Uses

[4 Spelling the string](#step-ours-string): $g=B+6$ and the spacers. [6 Two edges stay free](#step-ours-skeleton): (4). [8 Walls fix the ends](#step-ours-anchors): (2) and (5).

### Proof

Write $g=B+6$. Every separator fills a run of $g$ consecutive nodes of the bin edge by [8 Walls fix the ends](#step-ours-anchors) (2), and the runs of different separators are disjoint.

1. **Short joins.** Between the last H of one separator and the first H of the next, the string has $B+4$ P's: the rod's last P, the $B+2$ of the spacer and the next rod's first P. So these two H's are at most $B+5$ steps apart, and fewer than $g$ rows apart.
2. **One step of the induction.** Say a separator runs in increasing $j$ and ends at row $b$, so it covers rows $b-g+1$ to $b$. If the next separator lies below, both its ends are at row $b-g$ or lower, at least $g$ rows from $b$. If it lies above and runs in decreasing $j$, it starts at its top end, at row $b+g$ or higher. Both contradict step 1. So it lies above and runs in increasing $j$. The case of decreasing $j$ is the same, upside down.
3. **The whole train.** By induction the $K+1$ separators follow each other in string order, in one direction. So the first H of the first and the last H of the last are at least $(K+1)g-1>1$ rows apart. By [8 Walls fix the ends](#step-ours-anchors) (5) they are then $n-3$ rows apart, so they lie in rows 1 and $n-2$, the two ends of the bin edge. This proves (1) and (2).
4. **The gaps.** The nodes left are those between consecutive separators, $K$ runs, some possibly empty, with $(n-2)-(K+1)g=KB$ nodes in all. By [8 Walls fix the ends](#step-ours-anchors) (2) the item rods fill them. This proves (3).
