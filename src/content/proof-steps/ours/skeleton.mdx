### The idea

This step is the same as in the original proof ([6 Two edges stay free](../original-proof/#step-bl-skeleton)): our corner rod and tenth-edge rod are the original ones. The rods fill the edges and corners of the cube, 8 corners joined by 12 edges of $n-2$ nodes each, and each rod lies along them as a path ([5 The counts match](#step-ours-local-rules)).

A path along the edges turns only at corners. Between two corners it covers a whole edge, and before its first corner and after its last it covers at most one edge each. So a path through $r$ corners has at most $r+(r+1)(n-2)$ nodes. The corner rod has $9n-10=8+9(n-2)$, so it passes all 8 corners and covers 9 whole edges. At each corner it uses two of the three edges, so the three edges it misses share no corner. The tenth-edge rod fills one of them, and the other two stay empty: the free edges.

Every corner is now taken, so the two free edges are two separate runs of $n-2$ nodes. Each later rod is a path, so it lies on consecutive nodes of one free edge. This is what lets the items sit side by side in the gaps.

### Statement

The first two rods fill ten of the twelve edges, and the two edges left share no corner. Every later rod lies on consecutive nodes of one of them.

### Exact statement

Here an **edge** means the $n-2$ edge nodes between two corners. In a perfect fold:

1. the corner rod <Seq s="(PHP)^{9n−10}" /> covers all 8 corners and all nodes of 9 edges;
2. the tenth-edge rod <Seq s="(PHP)^{n−2}" /> fills one of the 3 edges left;
3. the other two, the **free edges**, share no corner;
4. every later rod, that is the single, the separators, the anchor rod and the item rods, fills a run of consecutive nodes of one free edge.

### Uses

[4 Spelling the string](#step-ours-string): the rod lengths. [5 The counts match](#step-ours-local-rules): (b), (d), and the fact that a rod's H's form a path in the skeleton.

### Proof

1. The skeleton consists of 12 paths of $n-2$ edge nodes, joined at 8 corners, with 3 edges at each corner. Two skeleton nodes are neighbours only along these paths.
2. A path in the skeleton that meets $r$ corners has at most $r+(r+1)(n-2)$ nodes. Between two consecutive corners it runs along a whole edge, and before the first and after the last corner it covers at most $n-2$ nodes each.
3. The corner rod is such a path with $9n-10=8+9(n-2)$ nodes. Since $r\le8$, step 2 forces $r=8$ and both end runs complete. So the rod covers the 8 corners, the 7 edges between consecutive corners, and the 2 edges of its end runs: 9 edges. Its two ends are not corners.
4. Every corner lies inside the path, so the rod uses two of its three edges there. So each corner meets at most one of the 3 edges left, and no two of them share a corner.
5. The tenth-edge rod is a path of $n-2$ lone H's on skeleton nodes not yet used: the nodes of the 3 edges left. Their corners are taken, so no node of one of these edges is a neighbour of a node of another. So the rod fills one of them.
6. What is left of the skeleton is the two free edges, two runs of $n-2$ nodes with no neighbours in common. A later rod is a path on these nodes, so it lies in one run and fills consecutive nodes of it.
